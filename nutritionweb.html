Nutrition

Important information about nutrition

Nutrients: Essential food compounds required for life, growth, and bodily repair.
Macronutrients: Large-scale nutrients that provide your body with energy in the form of calories.
Carbohydrates: The body's primary and fastest fuel source, providing 4 calories per gram.
Proteins: Structural building blocks made of amino acids that repair muscles and organs, providing 4 calories per gram.
Fats: Sources for cell health, hormone production, and nutrient absorption, providing 9 calories per gram.
Micronutrients: Low-dose vitamins and minerals required to keep your metabolism and organs running smoothly.
Vitamins: Organic compounds split into water-soluble and fat-soluble categories that regulate cellular processes.
Minerals: Inorganic elements necessary for structural bone health, nerve signals, and fluid balance.
Water: A zero-calorie essential that regulates body temperature and transports nutrients to cells.

Healthy Eating

Important information on healthy eating

Tips for eating healthy:
Eat regularly
Don’t skip breakfast
Eat with other people not the tv
This helps because eating with other people usually includes more food groups and makes you eat more regularly.

Building a balanced plate:
Half your plate should be fruits and veg, potatoes dot count.
A quarter of the plate should be wholegrains like brown rice, oats, whole-wheat pasta or whole grain bread.
The last quarter on the plate should be filled with healthy protein like fish, poultry, beans, lentils, nuts, seeds, eggs or tofu. And limit red meats like bacon and sausage.
Cooking with oils like avocado oil, extra virgin olive oil and canola oil are good because they are healthy fats.

Diet:
Drink lots of water, tea or coffee, less sugary drinks and energy drinks.
Cut back on sugars, sodium and saturated fats.
Watch how much you eat and try cook at home because you can see what goes in it.

Health

Important information on health

Physical Health: It can benefit your physical health by improving your bodies overall condition allowing you to perform usual day to day activities with fatigue and stress.
Mental Health: It can benefit your mental health by improving your wellbeing, reducing stress and improving your decision making.
Disease Prevention: It can improve your disease prevention by supplying your body with micronutrients and fibres that can help regulate your metabolism and support the immune system.
Benefits of eating certain food:
Berries: Support Health and Brain functions
Greens such as spinach: Protect eye health and lower blood levels
Almonds and Walnuts: Benefits your heart and brain.
Oily Fish: Reduce the risk of heart disease and support cellular health
Legumes such as beans: offer high dietary fibre to aid blood sugar control and gut health.
Avocados: Improve heart health

Lifestyle

Important information on Lifestyle

Adults need 7 to 9 hours of uninterrupted sleep.
Maintain a constant sleep schedule of sleeping and waking at similar if not the same times.
Avoid heavy meals, caffeine, nicotine and alcohol close to when going to sleep.
Turn off phones, laptops, and computers 1 to 2 hours before going to sleep as blue light can trick your brain into staying awake.
Exercising regularly can reduce stress. Moving your body for at least 30 minutes a day or aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity.
Focusing on what you can control can help lower stress. This can be done by denying extra plans, denying a drink or denying playing too many games.
Create screen free zones. These zones should be the bedroom, dining table and family gathering spots.
Decided on screentime timers beforehand and use the device timers to keep track of remaining screen time.
The four main habits are: Balanced nutrition, Regular physical activity, Quality sleep, Stress management and mental wellbeing.
